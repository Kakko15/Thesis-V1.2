IskAI Fidelity Audit

A joint engineering and academic review of A Centralized AI-Powered Thesis Library Using Retrieval-Augmented Generation (Barlis & Gallardo, BSCS Data Mining, ISU Echague) against the Thesis-V1 repository that implements it.

Verdict

The software is in unusually good shape for an undergraduate thesis. The RAG pipeline implements every numeric and structural claim the manuscript makes, the quality gates the paper names all run and all pass at HEAD, and the version tables are exact to the pinned dependency files. The paper, after eight correction passes, is faithful to the system it describes.

The research is not yet in the same state. Objective 2, the baseline-versus-RAG comparison that is the thesis's central empirical claim, has an excellent instrument and no formal result. The Golden Dataset is forty placeholders, the faculty panel has not validated anything, the 50-thesis governed corpus does not exist, and the only Ragas-scored artifact is a three-query smoke test from July run under a superseded configuration. Everything below sorts into “protect what is already right” and “close the research gap before the defense”.

System as built

Two services and a worker share one Supabase project. FastAPI (rag-thesis-backend) uses the service-role key, so no RLS policy constrains the backend; every authorization decision is made in Python. The React SPA (rag-thesis-frontend) holds only the anon key, and the effective RLS surface for it is the user's own profiles row plus the avatar bucket. The PDF bucket is private and carries a restrictive deny-all policy for anon and authenticated roles.

Objective 2 readiness

The comparison harness (evaluation/run_comparison.py) calls the RAG path in-process through _chat_impl, runs the baseline with the identical model, timeout, retry and thinking settings, routes both arms through the same key pool, checkpoints every query so an interrupted run resumes, and excludes provider outages instead of scoring them as wrong answers. It refuses to mark a result formal_result: true unless the dataset has 30–50 items, no placeholders, and three named panel members with ISO dates. That gate is currently doing its job by failing.

Every ground_truth and every source_thesis begins with REPLACE:. Category counts match the catalog exactly (Data Mining 9, WMAD 8, NETSEC 6, BSDSA 6, BSIS 4, Cross-track 3, Negative control 3, BLIS 1), so the instrument is designed; it is not filled.

DisclosedSame-family judge and shared embedding model

Ragas is driven by gemini-3.5-flash-lite judging gemini-3.6-flash, and Answer Correctness's semantic half uses the same gemini-embedding-001 the retriever indexes with.

Disclosed in the manuscript (3.2.4, through build_corrections.py): both models are named, the shared embedding space is stated, and it is framed as a limitation of the automated scores rather than a property of the architecture. The structural fact does not change — a cross-vendor judge would need a second provider — so the remaining mitigation, a small human-scored subsample to anchor the automated figures, is named in the text as still open.

MethodologyAnswer Correctness measures data access more than hallucination

Ground truths are derived from a private corpus the baseline cannot see, and the baseline prompt invites it to “cite specific theses if you can”. The paper's Objective 2 wording is “factual accuracy and hallucination mitigation”, but no hallucination or fabricated-citation rate is computed for the baseline, because Faithfulness needs contexts. Consider a simple manual count of fabricated citations per arm; it is cheap and it is the claim the introduction actually makes.

FixedNotices contaminated the RAG diagnostics

In the July smoke run, a “No relevant thesis” notice with five retrieved contexts was scored Faithfulness 0.0 and Context Precision 0.0 and averaged into means of 0.6 and 0.622. The three negative controls would have done the same whenever retrieval returned anything above 0.30.

Fixed in b74d872: every row now records whether the RAG arm answered or returned a notice, classified from the response object by the same call production makes before persisting rather than by re-matching the text afterwards. The diagnostics report answers and notices separately as well as pooled, and the pooled mean keeps its old meaning so the earlier artifacts stay comparable. Section 3.2.5 now states that the separated figures are the ones read as grounding and retrieval quality — which is the decision this finding asked for, made in the manuscript rather than left to be discovered in the output.

DesignCorpus-wide frequency questions cannot be answered from five chunks

Dataset items 3, 11, 18 and 37 ask “most commonly” or “most often”. With top-k 5 the RAG arm is structurally unable to answer them, so they will depress its score for a reason unrelated to hallucination. Either reword them as evidence questions or route them to the archive-inventory fast path and document that.

FixedNo effect size or confidence interval

The harness reported p-values and means only, so a result could be significant at 0.05 without a reader ever learning how large the difference was.

Fixed in b74d872: every comparison now carries the signed mean paired difference with a two-sided 95 % t interval, Cohen's dz, and the matched-pairs rank-biserial correlation for the Wilcoxon branch, where dz is weakest. Each statistic emits its own definition string, so no one has to guess which convention produced it, and 3.2.5 commits to reporting them. The timing was the point: added after a formal run, this would have meant running the whole comparison again at two live Gemini calls per query.

Defects and risks

Sorted by consequence. Each was confirmed by reading the cited lines during this review.

Backend and data layer

FixedCreating a department failed on every schema past 2026-07-25

POST /departments/ inserted only name, track_label, tracks. Migration 20260725 adds departments.code as NOT NULL with no default and a unique index, so on any project built the documented way every create was a not-null violation surfaced as an unhandled 500. DepartmentCreate carried neither code nor active; only code broke the insert, since active defaults to true in the column.

Fixed in 6162193 on fix/department-create-code-column, after this review. code is now an optional field — the admin UI sends only the three original fields — and is otherwise derived with the migration's own formula, so an API-created row matches a migrated one. An underivable or over-long derived code is a 422, and a collision on departments_code_uq is a 409 rather than a second 500.

routers/departments.py:43-48 · migrations/20260725_normalized_academic_catalog.sql:6-9

DataLegacy papers stay invisible until they are re-embedded

The provenance backfill labels every pre-provenance index models/gemini-embedding-2, while match_chunks filters on the configured gemini-embedding-001, so legacy papers do not appear in retrieval or duplication.

The label is not the bug, and must not be “corrected”. Those vectors really were produced by gemini-embedding-2 — the PI-03 smoke of 2026-07-25 certifies exactly that model — so the stamp is accurate provenance and the exclusion is a working guard. Cosine similarity between a -2 vector and an 001 query vector is meaningless; relabelling the rows would make stale-space vectors retrievable and silently corrupt the scores, Context Precision among them. .env.example:33-35 states this intent, and apply_paper enforces it: it refuses an index built by another model unless --allow-model-change is passed, then re-embeds rather than relabels. The four tests that name gemini-embedding-2 are correct as written; test_migration_and_reindex.py:94 is the guard on this behaviour, not a stale string.

Scope is narrower than it first appears. The backfill is insert … select … from chunks, so on a fresh project it inserts no rows and there is nothing to exclude. Only a project that already held chunks when 20260720 was applied is affected, and the remedy is the documented reindex in README step 1.5.

supabase_setup.sql:354 · migrations/20260720_index_embedding_provenance.sql:48 · services/index_provenance.py:37 · scripts/reindex_citations.py:162-180

FixedHNSW post-filtering could under-return

match_chunks applies six equality filters and the threshold after an HNSW ordered scan. pgvector returns at most ef_search (default 40) candidates before the WHERE runs, so with a selective department or provenance filter it can return fewer than five rows, or none, while qualifying chunks exist. Nothing sets hnsw.ef_search. This matters directly to Context Precision.

Fixed in 47e8a7d: both retrieval RPCs now carry set hnsw.ef_search = 100, in the base schema and in 20260903_hnsw_ef_search.sql. check_topic_duplication was included as well — not flagged above, but it has the same exposure, so a manuscript above the 0.85 threshold could be missed outright. Not hnsw.iterative_scan, which is the cleaner fix but needs pgvector 0.8.0 while Table 3 says 0.7.0 and the live version is Supabase-managed and recorded nowhere. Guarded in both schema sources; not verified against a live database.

supabase_setup.sql:311-312, 873-887 · migrations/20260903_hnsw_ef_search.sql

PerformanceOne RPC per chunk in both duplication paths

Ingest screening and the novelty scanner call match_chunks once per chunk, serially. A 500-page manuscript is hundreds of round trips inside one 120-second lease, kept alive only by the heartbeat thread.

Deliberately not changed. This first read as a straightforward optimisation. It is not: routers/duplication.py:115-127 already records the decision — “batching the round trips is deliberately deferred because that would alter a scored pipeline stage” — and test_schema_consistency.py gives the same reason for leaving check_topic_duplication alone. A LATERAL batch would also let the planner move from an approximate HNSW scan to an exact one, which changes output. If the wall-clock cost has to be addressed before the baseline is locked, issuing the existing per-chunk calls concurrently preserves the SQL, the plan and the results exactly.

services/novelty.py:91-98 · routers/duplication.py:131-138

The README's replay warning about 20260718 is confirmed and is actually broader: replaying either 20260717 file also downgrades activate_paper_index and prune_inactive_indexes.

Manuscript notes

These are editorial, not substantive. The correction pipeline (build_corrections.py) regenerates the DOCX from the untouched original, which is exactly right; the PDF is exported by hand afterwards, and it had been left a day behind the DOCX — exactly that drift. Both were regenerated together in a1893b1 and are now 60 pages carrying the same corrections.

  • Reference-list “et al.” — still open. Bevara, Doliente, Gokdemir and Wong are listed as “et al.” in the reference list. APA 7 requires all authors up to twenty; “et al.” belongs in the in-text citation only. Deliberately not auto-corrected: the author lists have to come from the sources themselves, not from a lookup. Checking them surfaced two further problems — the Wong entry gives the wrong first-author initial (arXiv:2502.14898 is Lionel Wong, not “Wong, K.”), and the Gokdemir entry’s venue and DOI do not match the paper of that title, which publishes as PASC ’25 (10.1145/3732775.3733586) rather than CCGrid. Gokdemir also carries 24 authors, which changes the APA form again.
  • Inconsistent citation — fixed in a1893b1. Li and colleagues were cited as “Li et al. (2025)” in 2.1.5 and as “Li, 2025” in 2.1.7 for the same six-author paper.
  • Figures 3–5 still carry internal titles “Figure 1.1 / 1.2 / 1.3” while captioned Figure 3, 4, 5 (already logged as open in CORRECTIONS_APPLIED.md).
  • Table 2 row order. Python v3.14.7 sits after python-dotenv; move it to the top of the backend table.
  • Table 5 has a “Gap / Feasible” column with no explanation of what gap is being assessed.
  • Table 1 omits the Supabase JavaScript client, which carries the entire authentication flow, while listing Framer Motion. Table 4 omits Trivy, pip-audit, npm audit and Gitleaks although 3.2.5 promises “vulnerability counts identified through security scanning”.
  • Page 12 is two-thirds blank before Chapter 2.
  • 3.2.4 Reliability — fixed in a1893b1. “Memory leaks” is gone from the list of what SonarQube detects; its Python and JavaScript analysers do not detect them, and the reliability rating they produce is driven by bugs.
  • Pylint profile — fixed in a1893b1. The methodology now names .pylintrc: the line limit is raised to 120, the test suite is excluded, and exactly fourteen message families are disabled. There is no fail-under, so the score is informational rather than a gate.
  • Chapter 4 does not exist yet; when it is written, the reporting discipline in 3.2.5 (means across iterations) should be applied to figures generated on the final commit, not to the July and August runs.

Priority plan

In order. The first four are the difference between a proposal and a defensible study; the rest protect the engineering claims already made.

  1. Secure the four approvals and record them in the corpus manifest. Nothing downstream can be formal without them.
